# Self-Modifying AI Company Research

A long-horizon research and company-building program exploring adaptive, self-improving, self-modifying AI systems and the path from an initial commercial wedge to a major technology platform.

## Research Mission

Investigate what can be built today, what remains research-grade, and how a startup can develop defensible technology around:

- Self-improving and self-modifying agents
- Adaptive models and model scaffolds
- Self-modifying memory and databases
- Self-evolving websites and applications
- Autonomous software engineering and DevOps
- Agent evaluation, verification, observability, security and governance
- Recursive improvement and automated experimentation
- Agent runtimes, SDKs and developer ecosystems
- AI-native infrastructure and enterprise autonomous workflows

## Company Thesis

The research should identify a commercially valuable wedge that can evolve into an adaptive-intelligence platform rather than assuming that a universal self-modifying agent is the correct first product.

The program also studies how durable technology companies built platforms, ecosystems, infrastructure and moats, using companies such as NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google/DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon/AWS, Meta, Apple, Tesla, SpaceX, Databricks and other important companies as reference cases.

## Long-Term Direction

Near-term commercial technology should be evaluated as a potential practical substrate for the user's longer-term Cognitive Holobiont research. The Holobiont hypothesis is not assumed to be proven; connections must be technically justified by evidence.

## Repository Structure

- `research/` — literature, technical findings and field maps
- `experiments/` — experiments, benchmarks and results
- `architecture/` — system designs and technical decisions
- `company/` — market, product, business model and commercialization research
- `competitors/` — company and platform analysis
- `holobiont-connection/` — technically justified connections to the Holobiont program
- `research-log/` — dated cumulative research logs

## Research Principles

1. Prefer primary and high-quality sources.
2. Follow citation chains and search for contradictory evidence.
3. Separate demonstrated capability from speculation.
4. Record failures and negative results, not only successes.
5. Test claims experimentally where possible.
6. Treat safety, security, rollback, provenance and governance as core architecture.
7. Preserve prior findings and revise them when stronger evidence appears.
8. Optimize for both scientific validity and commercial usefulness.

## Daily Program

Research is intended to accumulate continuously, with meaningful findings persisted here and reviewed as part of the daily 6:00 PM research cycle.
