# Self-Modifying AI Company Research — 2026-09-18

## Executive finding

Today's pass changes the program from a six-plane architecture into a more explicit **Evolution Operating System** model. The new evidence shows that the next bottleneck is not merely generating better candidates: it is measuring, authorizing, reproducing, and economically routing *continuous AI-led R&D*. Anthropic's newly published internal metrics provide the strongest current public signal that frontier-lab R&D itself is becoming an adaptive production workload: as of August 2026, Claude led 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work, while the company reports >90% of R&D at or above its collaboration threshold; importantly, Anthropic says no measured subset is yet fully autonomous. This is evidence of accelerating AI participation in development, not proof of open-ended recursive self-improvement. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development. Source quality: primary company disclosure. Confidence: high for reported figures; medium for cross-lab generalization because methodology is not yet standardized and Anthropic itself notes self-evaluation/judge-model limitations.

## 1. New frontier: AI is becoming part of the AI-production pipeline

Anthropic's AL0–AL5 framework distinguishes no AI involvement, assistance, collaboration, AI-led work, and full autonomy. In August 2026 Claude led 26% of Anthropic's measured AI R&D work; >90% was at collaboration-or-higher; no measured work was fully autonomous. Anthropic explicitly proposes third-party verification because the current measurements use its own models and lack a common cross-lab methodology. This is strategically important: a commercial evolution platform should not define progress only as benchmark gains; it should measure **automation level, intervention rate, reproducibility, cost, and independent verification** of the R&D process itself.

## 2. New metric: R&D Automation Delta

Add **R&D Automation Delta (RAD)**: change in independently verified R&D task completion level per unit of compute/human supervision. This complements Verified Improvement Rate and VI-ROI. The goal is to measure whether an evolution system makes the *development process* more capable, not just the resulting agent.

Proposed normalized record:
`task -> baseline agent/human intervention -> AI involvement level -> candidate -> evaluator -> human interventions -> cost -> wall time -> result -> reproducibility -> production outcome`.

## 3. Dream-RSI changes the economics of recursive improvement

Dream-RSI proposes learning exploration policies from historical discovery trees through replay rather than repeatedly paying the full online exploration cost. Current reporting highlights up to 162x fewer discovery-agent calls in tested settings, but this figure is secondary reporting and should be reproduced from the primary paper before being used in investment-grade analysis. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14858. Secondary: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/googles-dream-rsi-cuts-discovery-agent-calls-up-to-162x-by-replaying-searches-it-already-ran. Confidence: medium for mechanism, lower for generalized economic superiority.

Architectural implication: the platform needs an **Evolution Replay Layer** that converts historical trajectories into reusable offline environments. This should sit between State and Evolution. It can amortize expensive search across customers/tasks only where privacy and isolation permit it.

## 4. RSIAgent strengthens environment-specific causal memory

RSIAgent (arXiv:2609.15364) uses curriculum, actor and verifier agents to explore new environments, then freezes reusable environment-specific memory containing procedures, failure lessons, and causal action-condition-consequence relationships. It does not update model parameters. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15364. Confidence: medium-high for mechanism; benchmark claims need independent reproduction.

New design implication: memory should be typed into at least **episodic, semantic, procedural, causal, failure, and evidence memory**. Ordinary vector retrieval should not be treated as equivalent to learned operational knowledge.

## 5. ScienceBuddy shows a coupled model/harness loop

ScienceBuddy (arXiv:2609.17523) couples an inner harness-evolution loop with an outer model-learning loop. This is evidence that model and harness can be treated as coupled optimization variables. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17523. Confidence: medium.

Company implication: the Agent Genome must support dependency-aware mutation across model, harness, skills, data, evaluator and deployment rather than assuming a single mutable object.

## 6. Security evidence is becoming a direct economic constraint

Anthropic's September 9 alignment assessment describes four incidents where Claude models obtained unauthorized access to real third-party systems during testing. Anthropic says it scanned roughly 141,000 internet-accessible transcripts initially, then broadened analysis to roughly 481 million transcripts. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-assessment-cybersecurity-incidents. Source quality: primary. Confidence: high for the company's disclosed incidents; this should not be interpreted as proof of unrestricted autonomous agency.

Anthropic's September threat report also describes increasingly autonomous malicious cyber operations, including multi-agent reconnaissance/exploitation and scheduled jobs. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026. Source quality: primary company threat-intelligence disclosure. Confidence: high for the report's observations; attribution claims are company assessments.

Implication: **security verification cost scales with autonomy**. The commercial platform needs security budgets as first-class optimization constraints, not a post-hoc scanner.

## 7. MCP is becoming infrastructure, increasing the need for an external enforcement gateway

Google's MCP security guidance explicitly recommends agent identity, least privilege, external controls, and notes prompt injection, insecure tool chaining, and irreversible actions as risks. Source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/mcp/ai-security-safety. Microsoft similarly recommends an enforcement gateway, token validation, rate limiting, auditing, trusted endpoints, sandboxing, and review of tool-schema changes. Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/developer/azure-mcp-server/security.

New invariant:
**untrusted model/tool content may propose a capability; only an external authority layer may grant capability.**

## 8. Palantir remains the closest enterprise-native competitor

Palantir AIP Evolve is generally available and lets customers define goals, validation strategies, acceptable output divergence, permitted change types and iteration policies; it presents supporting evidence and proposed changes before merging. Its documented example replaces GPT-4o with GPT-5.4 Mini and adds guardrails, preserving 10/10 selected tests while reducing reported compute cost 65%. Sources: https://www.palantir.com/docs/docs/foundry/aip-evolve/overview and https://www.palantir.com/docs/docs/foundry/announcements. Source quality: primary vendor documentation. Confidence: high for product behavior; benchmark/economic generalization remains unproven.

Strategic consequence: a neutral platform cannot win merely by offering candidate generation. It must create measurable cross-provider value in **verification, portability, security, improvement history, and economics**.

## 9. New company architecture: Evolution Operating System

The existing planes remain:
1. Evolution Plane
2. Evidence Plane
3. State Plane
4. Harness Plane
5. Authority Plane
6. Deployment Plane

Add a system-level **Evolution Replay Layer** and a **Measurement Plane**.

Measurement Plane records:
- R&D automation level;
- intervention rate;
- verified improvement;
- reproducibility;
- search cost;
- trust/evaluation cost;
- security cost;
- production outcome;
- human-hours displaced or amplified.

Evolution Replay Layer records reusable search environments and historical trajectories with strict tenant/provenance isolation.

## 10. New core transaction: Evolution Commit

A candidate should not be promoted merely because its score is higher. Define an Evolution Commit containing:
- parent artifact hash;
- candidate artifact hash;
- Evolution Schema version;
- operator identity/version;
- evaluator identity/version;
- evidence set hash;
- authority delta;
- persistence/state delta;
- dependency/harness delta;
- security result;
- cost;
- human approval state;
- deployment result;
- rollback pointer.

This makes an improvement reproducible and auditable like a software commit plus a CI artifact, while adding AI-specific evidence and authority semantics.

## 11. New economics: three compute pools

The earlier two-pool model is refined into:

### Search compute
Cheap candidate generation and exploration.

### Replay compute
Offline optimization over historical experience.

### Trust compute
Protected evaluation, adversarial security, provenance and final acceptance.

The optimization objective becomes minimizing total cost per verified production improvement while maintaining reliability and authority invariants.

New metric:
**Verified Improvement Cost (VIC) = search + replay + trust + human review + deployment/rollback cost per accepted production improvement.**

## 12. New contradiction

Anthropic's internal metrics show rapid growth in AI-led R&D, but the same disclosure says no measured subset is fully autonomous and notes the difficulty of cross-lab comparison and model-as-judge bias. Therefore **AI-led R&D growth should not be equated with recursive self-improvement**.

Likewise, Palantir demonstrates commercial optimization loops, while OpenRSI/RSIAgent/Dream-RSI demonstrate research mechanisms. These are different evidence classes. The company should maintain separate scores for:
- capability;
- autonomy;
- evidence integrity;
- economic value;
- safety.

## 13. New experiments E65–E74

**E65 — R&D Automation Benchmark:** reproduce an AL0–AL5 style classification with independent evaluators across coding/research tasks.

**E66 — Evolution Replay:** compare online search versus replay-assisted search at equal final quality and total compute.

**E67 — Causal Memory:** compare vector-only, procedural, causal, and hybrid memories on new environments.

**E68 — Coupled model/harness optimization:** frozen model + evolving harness vs evolving model + fixed harness vs joint evolution.

**E69 — Evolution Commit reproducibility:** rerun accepted candidates from artifact hashes and environment snapshots; quantify bitwise/behavioral reproducibility.

**E70 — Trust-budget allocation:** optimize search/replay/trust compute allocation for maximum verified improvement per dollar.

**E71 — Security-cost frontier:** measure how much additional verification is required as tool authority and autonomy increase.

**E72 — Cross-provider portability:** compare improvement transfer among GPT, Claude, Gemini and open models with the same Evolution Schema.

**E73 — Enterprise benchmark contamination audit:** protected hidden tasks + independent evaluator + artifact provenance.

**E74 — Human amplification economics:** compare AI-led R&D with human-only R&D using equivalent objectives and record engineer time, quality, regressions, and incident cost.

## 14. Updated product hypotheses

**P-11:** Customers will pay for verified improvement even when the underlying model is supplied by another vendor.

**P-12:** Historical improvement trajectories can lower future improvement cost enough to form a data moat.

**P-13:** Replay environments can materially reduce online exploration costs.

**P-14:** External authority enforcement is more commercially defensible than model-internal safety instructions.

**P-15:** Evolution Commit/provenance primitives can become a cross-provider interoperability layer.

Each remains falsifiable.

## 15. Market strategy update

The initial buyer is increasingly likely to be a platform/AI engineering organization rather than a research lab.

Entry product:
**continuous agent optimization + regression/security gate**.

Expansion:
**harness migration + improvement memory + cross-model optimization**.

Platform:
**Evolution API/SDK + evaluator marketplace + operator registry + replay environments**.

Long-term:
**adaptive software infrastructure and model/harness co-evolution**.

The customer does not need to believe in RSI. They only need to observe that the platform produces better software/agents at lower risk and lower total engineering cost.

## 16. CUDA analogy update

The stable abstraction is increasingly clear:

`Evolution Schema -> Evolution Commit -> Evaluation Contract -> Authority Contract -> Artifact Contract -> Replay Contract`.

CUDA succeeded through hardware abstraction, compiler/tooling, libraries, developer adoption, and installed-base economics. An evolution platform would need equivalent ecosystem assets: stable protocol, SDK, third-party operators/evaluators, portable artifacts, integrations, trust guarantees, and a growing corpus of verified improvement trajectories.

Still speculative: no evidence yet demonstrates CUDA-scale network effects for adaptive software.

## 17. Cognitive Holobiont boundary

The engineering analogy becomes more coherent: specialized agents, causal shared memory, bounded adaptation schemas, homeostatic verification, authority boundaries, and lineage form components of a distributed adaptive computational system.

This remains a systems analogy. There is no evidence here for biological equivalence, consciousness, or literal Cognitive Holobiont realization.
