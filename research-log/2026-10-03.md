# 2026-10-03 — Deep Research Pass

## New evidence

### 1. Reliability must become a first-class evolution objective
Fresh 2026 evidence continues to show a large gap between agent capability and production reliability. A new reliability benchmark, ThinkingBox-Bench, reports a large discovery–reliability gap: pass@1 can be materially higher than the probability of succeeding on every repeated run. This supports measuring pass^N / repeat reliability rather than only pass@1. Source quality: secondary/open benchmark report; confidence: medium pending primary-paper verification.

A separate production-focused synthesis reports 41%–86.7% task failure across seven multi-agent frameworks and attributes a substantial fraction to system-design failures such as repetition and termination. Treat this as secondary evidence, not a definitive industry-wide statistic.

### 2. Evaluation and observability are different control loops
Current practitioner evidence indicates that tracing is widespread while systematic evaluation is less widespread. Architectural consequence: the Evolution Runtime must distinguish telemetry/observability from acceptance evidence. Traces explain what happened; evaluators establish whether a change improved the intended outcome.

### 3. Harness evolution remains the strongest demonstrated RSI substrate
Recursive Harness Self-Improvement reports substantial gains and up to 60% inference-cost reduction on 30 synthetic ML-research tasks, with gains attributed mainly to context management/inter-agent information flow. Source: arXiv preprint, 2026-07-17; confidence: promising, not general proof.

### 4. MCP/tool security is an evolution-boundary problem
MCP-ITP reports up to 84.2% attack success against tested agents while evading detection in its experimental setting. A separate 2026 MCP threat-model study identifies tool poisoning and insufficient client-side validation as major risks. These are research results, not evidence that all MCP deployments are insecure. Architectural consequence: tool metadata, schemas and outputs remain untrusted inputs; authorization must be external to the evolving agent.

### 5. Commercial validation
Palantir AIP Evolve is generally available and coordinates AI FDE agents to optimize AI systems under goals, validation strategies and operational constraints. Its public example uses ten selected test cases and reports a 65% compute reduction while maintaining those tests. This validates demand for governed AI improvement, but the validation set is too small to establish generalization. Source quality: vendor primary; confidence: high for product behavior, low for generalized performance claims.

## Changed conclusions

1. **Reliability is now a promotion metric, not merely an observability metric.**
2. **Repeatability must be measured explicitly.** Add pass^N / repeated-success metrics to the acceptance protocol.
3. **Search-visible tests and acceptance-hidden tests must be separate.**
4. **Production traces should feed diagnosis and benchmark generation, but never directly grant promotion authority.**
5. **MCP is an interoperability layer, not a trust boundary.**

## Architecture amendment

Evolution protocol:

Snapshot → Scope → Generate → Regularize → Sandbox → Search-Evaluate → Hidden Acceptance → Security Verify → EvolutionCommit → Canary → Production Outcome → Replay

New required dimensions in EvolutionCommit:
- single-run performance
- repeated-run reliability
- OOD retention
- security result
- authority delta
- cost delta
- provenance/replay status
