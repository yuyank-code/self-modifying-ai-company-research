# Deep Research Pass — 2026-09-05

## Executive finding

The strongest new evidence today materially changes the competitive thesis: the "self-improving AI" category is moving from research prototypes toward productized, governed improvement loops. Palantir's August 18, 2026 AIP Evolve beta is especially important: it coordinates fleets of AI FDE agents to improve AI systems, accepts an explicit optimization target, validation strategy, and operational constraints, and requires review before merging changes. This is close to the previously hypothesized Improvement Control Plane. It means our opportunity cannot simply be "build self-improving agents"; we need a sharper wedge, stronger independent evaluation, cross-platform portability, or a differentiated data/evolution layer. Source quality: primary vendor documentation; confidence high for feature existence, lower for performance claims because early-adopter results are vendor-reported.

## 1. Competitive reality: self-improvement is becoming a product feature

Palantir AIP Evolve (Aug. 18, 2026) supports iterative improvement for model migration, cost reduction, latency reduction, eval-score improvement and custom goals. It lets users choose validation suites, scoring criteria, acceptable output divergence, permitted change types and iteration limits. It produces proposed changes, validation results, output comparisons, evidence and confidence, and integrates with branching/review before merge. This is almost a direct implementation of the controlled evolution loop proposed in earlier research.

Primary source: https://www.palantir.com/docs/docs/foundry/announcements

Strategic implication: "AI that improves itself" is no longer sufficient differentiation. The company thesis must move toward a substrate that is portable across model vendors and enterprise stacks, or own a higher-order primitive such as trusted improvement measurement, cross-system evolution history, or an open improvement protocol.

## 2. OpenAI independently validates the same basic loop

OpenAI's May 27, 2026 description of self-improving tax agents uses a three-part loop: practitioner correction reveals a failure; production traces turn corrections into evaluations; the resulting eval becomes an optimization target for Codex. This is strong evidence that real-world corrections can be converted into durable improvement signals.

Primary source: https://openai.com/index/building-self-improving-tax-agents-with-codex/

Strategic implication: the highest-value data asset may not be raw conversations. It may be the transformation from production failure -> evaluation -> attempted fix -> measured outcome. That sequence should be treated as an Improvement Trajectory and stored as structured provenance.

## 3. The market is already separating infrastructure layers

The 2026 agent-infrastructure landscape shows distinct categories for durable execution, observability/evaluation, gateways and memory. This means an undifferentiated "agent platform" is structurally crowded. Presenc AI reports Temporal as a major durable-execution layer and Braintrust/LangSmith/Langfuse/Helicone/AgentOps/Phoenix across evaluation/observability; treat market figures as secondary-source estimates rather than audited facts.

Secondary source: https://presenc.ai/research/ai-agent-infrastructure-startups-2026

Strategic implication: our product must own an operation that existing layers do not: controlled change generation + independent verification + promotion + longitudinal measurement.

## 7. A new research-agent result strengthens the recursive-improvement case

AREX (July 23, 2026) implements recursive self-improvement for deep research by alternating an inner research loop with an outer audit loop. It explicitly exploits a discovery-verification asymmetry: finding answers can be hard while checking constraints can be easier. It also learns a context-update tool to compress long interaction histories into a compact improvement state while preserving verified evidence and unresolved constraints.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21461

Strategic implication: the improvement engine may need two fundamentally different subsystems: a creative candidate generator and a conservative verifier/auditor. Treating both as one model is a major design risk.

## 9. Memory is likely to become a platform feature, not a standalone moat

Current 2026 market mapping shows multiple memory architectures: user/session/agent scoped stores, OS-inspired memory layers, and temporal/episodic memory. Hyperscalers are also integrating persistent memory into their agent platforms. Treat market numbers from vendor/industry maps cautiously.

Secondary source: https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/08/ai-agent-infrastructure-consolidation-map-2026-picks-shovels

Strategic implication: adaptive memory alone is probably not the company. The stronger idea is to treat memory changes as one class of mutations inside the larger improvement protocol.

## 10. Self-modification taxonomy updated

The practical capability ladder should now be treated as ten distinct mutation surfaces:

1. Prompt/instruction mutation — high feasibility.
2. Memory/skill mutation — high feasibility.
3. Tool selection/routing mutation — high feasibility.
4. Workflow/controller mutation — high-to-medium feasibility.
5. Source-code/harness mutation — medium-to-high feasibility in sandboxes.
6. Infrastructure/configuration mutation — medium feasibility with strong controls.
7. Database/retrieval mutation — medium feasibility; correctness constraints dominate.
8. Model-component/fine-tuning mutation — medium-to-low feasibility depending on compute/data.
9. Weight-level continual adaptation — technically possible but operationally expensive and evaluation-sensitive.
10. Open-ended successor redesign — no reliable general demonstration; research hypothesis only.

Commercial priority should initially be 1-5, because those provide a large improvement surface without requiring frontier-scale model training.

## 11. New architectural requirement: Capability Delta

The previous Agent Genome concept needs one addition: every candidate improvement should produce a **Capability Delta** describing what the candidate can do that the parent cannot.

For example:
- new tool permission
- broader filesystem access
- network access
- database write access
- ability to alter evaluator configuration
- ability to alter deployment configuration
- ability to invoke another agent

The candidate should be evaluated twice:

A. Does behavior improve?
B. Does capability increase beyond what policy permits?

A candidate can therefore be rejected even when its task score improves.

This creates a separation between **performance promotion** and **authority promotion**.

## 12. New security principle: evaluator immutability boundary

The strongest architecture should make the evaluation substrate a protected control plane. A candidate may propose a new evaluator, but cannot silently replace the authoritative evaluator that decides whether it gets promoted.

Recommended hierarchy:
- candidate-generated tests: advisory
- developer-owned tests: protected
- hidden regression tests: inaccessible
- security policy tests: protected
- promotion policy: protected
- audit/provenance: append-only

This is analogous to separating application code from the CI/CD signing and deployment authority.

## 13. Proposed benchmark: Evolution Reliability Benchmark (ERB)

A future benchmark should score an adaptive system across six axes:

1. Improvement: delta in task success.
2. Persistence: improvement retained on future tasks.
3. Generalization: improvement on unseen task distributions.
4. Safety: security/regression violations.
5. Efficiency: improvement per dollar/token/second.
6. Governance: unauthorized capability changes and audit completeness.

A seventh metric should track evaluator integrity:

7. Measurement Robustness: performance under hidden, adversarial and independently generated evaluations.

A system that gains +10 points on visible evals but +0 on hidden evals should not count as a successful self-improvement system.

## 16. Company moat revised

The moat should not be "we have an AI that improves itself." That is reproducible.

Potential durable assets:
- longitudinal Improvement Graphs
- high-quality hidden evaluation suites

- verified improvement operators
- Capability Delta policies

The most strategically valuable dataset may become the mapping:

**system state + environment + failure + attempted mutation + evaluation + real-world outcome**

This is much richer than generic interaction logs.

## 17. Major contradiction to track

Palantir AIP Evolve demonstrates that the exact improvement-control-plane concept can be built by a large vertically integrated enterprise platform. Microsoft Foundry and OpenAI are also moving toward governed continuous improvement. Therefore, the hypothesis that "self-improvement infrastructure is an uncontested greenfield" is false.

The remaining opportunity must survive the hyperscaler/platform threat.

Potential defensible position:
- model/vendor neutrality
- open protocol
- independent verification
- cross-platform Improvement Graph
- specialized safety/evolution research
- developer ecosystem outside any one cloud
- benchmark authority

## 18. Business test

The first customer value proposition should not be "your agent can self-modify."

It should be:

**"We reduce the engineering work required to maintain and improve production agents, and we prove the improvement."**

Primary economic metrics:
- engineer-hours avoided/month
- success-rate increase
- intervention reduction
- cost reduction
- latency reduction
- incident reduction
- time-to-fix recurring failures

A pilot should establish a baseline for these before any adaptive loop is deployed.

## 19. Experiment backlog update

E1 — Static vs adaptive coding agent.

E2 — Prompt-only vs memory-only vs workflow-only vs code-level adaptation.

E3 — Same improvement engine with shared vs independent evaluator.

E4 — Visible eval vs hidden eval to measure evaluator gaming.

E5 — Improvement with and without Capability Delta enforcement.

E6 — Cross-model adaptation: same improvement artifact applied to two model providers.

E7 — Cost-normalized improvement: compare extra inference/training spend against persistent gains.

E8 — Improvement trajectory compression: determine whether failure->fix->outcome histories predict future useful mutations.

E9 — Sandbox-to-production transfer: measure whether improvements discovered in synthetic environments survive real workloads.

E10 — Longitudinal degradation: run an adaptive system for many iterations and measure regression, diversity collapse, evaluator overfitting and capability creep.

## 20. Cognitive Holobiont connection

Today's evidence supports only a constrained analogy: a future population of specialized adaptive agents could share improvement infrastructure, memory, communication and quality-control mechanisms. This resembles distributed specialization and homeostatic regulation.

It does not yet establish the stronger claim that such a system constitutes a cognitive holobiont. That remains a separate research hypothesis requiring explicit definitions and empirical tests.
