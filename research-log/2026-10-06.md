# Deep Research Log — 2026-10-06

## Executive conclusion

The research program now has two distinct bottlenecks: **evidence portability** and **evolution economics**. Recent evidence strengthens bounded harness evolution, but the commercial platform must prove that an improvement remains trustworthy when model, runtime, evaluator, tools, and benchmark versions change independently.

## New evidence

### 1. Regularized harness evolution
RRSI reports up to 14.1 points of in-distribution gain and 4.7 points across five OOD benchmarks, while reducing policy tokens by 30% versus unregularized evolution. The work explicitly identifies overfitting of evolution tasks as a failure mode. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972. Quality: primary preprint; confidence: medium-high for the reported experiments, lower for generalization.

### 2. Self-evolving harness with one frozen model
A Sept. 29 preprint reports a frozen model alternately solving and proposing harness edits, with a 49-line seed harness and multi-domain evolution. It reports +4.48 average points in-distribution and +12.64 OOD after the first stage. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38372. Quality: primary preprint; confidence: medium; replication required.

### 3. Reliability must be a promotion metric
ThinkingBox reports 65.36% pass@1 but only 25.25% pass^20 on 507 stateful tasks, exposing a large discovery-versus-reliability gap. Source: https://nerdleveltech.com/ai-agent-reliability-benchmark-thinkingbox. Quality: secondary report of an open benchmark; confidence: medium. The benchmark should be independently inspected before treating the exact figures as canonical.

### 4. Commercial evolution is emerging
Palantir AIP Evolve is generally available and coordinates AI FDE agents to optimize AI systems under explicit goals, validation strategies, constraints and iteration limits. Its public example uses 10 representative tests and reports 65% lower compute cost while retaining those selected tests. Source: https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/aip-evolve/overview. Quality: primary vendor documentation; confidence: high for product behavior, low-medium for generalizable performance claims.

### 5. MCP tool poisoning remains a material authority-boundary problem
MCP-ITP reports up to 84.2% attack success in its experimental implicit-tool-poisoning setup while suppressing malicious-tool detection. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07395. Quality: primary preprint; confidence: medium for the benchmarked setup, not evidence that all MCP systems are similarly vulnerable. A separate April 2026 study proposes eight detection checks including hidden instructions, excessive permissions, exfiltration endpoints and rug-pull behavior. Source: https://www.qcrypton.com/publications/mcp-tool-poisoning. Quality: technical publication; confidence: medium.

## Changed conclusions

1. **Evolution evidence must be portable but conditional.** An evidence record is not valid merely because its benchmark name matches. Validity depends on model, harness, runtime, tool, evaluator, dataset and benchmark versions.
2. **Memory/skill persistence belongs under the same commit discipline as code evolution.** Durable state can alter future behavior and authority.
3. **The company should not own the lowest-level sandbox by default.** It should integrate with security substrates and own the evolution/evidence protocol above them.
4. **Reliability under repetition is a first-class objective.** pass@1 is discovery evidence; pass^N or equivalent repeated-trial measures are reliability evidence.
5. **Search evaluation and acceptance evaluation must be separate.** Optimizer-visible tests are for exploration; hidden/independent evaluation determines promotion.
6. **Commercial wedge:** trusted adaptive software change control, beginning with agent/harness optimization and expanding toward a portable Evolution Runtime.

## Economics hypothesis

A useful platform metric should be **Verified Improvement Yield (VIY)**:

VIY = verified production value delta / total evolution cost

where total evolution cost includes generation, search, evaluator, replay, security verification, deployment and rollback overhead.

A second metric is **Trust Compute Efficiency (TCE)**:

TCE = verified improvement / verification+search compute

The product must show that governance does not consume all economic value created by optimization.
