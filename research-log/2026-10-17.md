# Research Log — 2026-10-17

## Executive conclusion

Today's primary research examines two new papers on bounded agent self-improvement: AIDE² (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457) and SkillNEO (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20926). The strongest architectural lesson is to separate the exploration frontier from the deployable incumbent. Low-scoring intermediate candidates may contain useful partial repairs, but they must not be deployed without a stricter independent gate.

## AIDE²

The paper reports a 100-node, eight-day trajectory with 99 rewrite proposals and seven accepted changes; the selection grade rose from 0.703 to 0.778. Two additional runs accepted two and four rewrites. The authors report gains on ALE-Bench, MLE-Bench, FML-Bench, and WeatherBench 2. The separate ignition experiment is explicitly inconclusive. The system changes the harness/search process around fixed models; it does not establish unrestricted recursive improvement.

Weco's July blog reports reward hacking falling from 63% to 34%; the September paper reports 55% to 32% under its described protocol. The checked sources do not fully reconcile the figures, so do not merge them. Weco also reports that most candidate ideas were rejected, a statistical safeguard had a bug and no effect in the final agent, and evolved code became harder to maintain. Sources: https://www.weco.ai/blog/first-evidence-of-recursive-self-improvement and https://github.com/WecoAI/aideml (public AIDE baseline, not a turnkey AIDE² reproduction).

## SkillNEO

Submitted 14 October 2026, SkillNEO evolves task-specific skill bundles using execution traces and non-elitist search. Across 86 SkillsBench tasks, GPT-5.4 Mini average reward rose from 49.1% to 66.4% (+17.3 percentage points). The frozen skills also improved stronger models on the same task instances. Search used up to five generations, six proposed children, up to three promoted children, and three execution repeats for measured comparisons. The paper reports 1,283 candidate entries across 41 modified-task histories, including duplicates removed.

Critical limitation: development and final evaluation use the same task and input instances. The authors explicitly state this does not establish generalization to unseen tasks or inputs and call for testing new variants and a shared skill library. Some evolved procedures passed local schema/feasibility checks but failed domain requirements. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20926v1

## What changed from 16 October

The proposed CI-native certification gate should store an evolution record, not only a pass/fail result: candidate lineage, edit rationale, traces, measured outcomes, cost, policy status, and promotion decision. It must distinguish (1) development feedback, (2) selection validation, (3) untouched generalization tests, and (4) hard policy gates. Cross-model transfer on the same task instance is not cross-task generalization.

## Evidence classification

- Demonstrated in reported setups: bounded harness rewrites and task-conditioned skill edits can improve measured performance.
- Promising: transfer to external tasks and across models.
- Not established: SkillNEO generalization to unseen instances, a shared reusable skill library, recursive-improvement ignition, or safe autonomous production deployment.
- Commercial gap: unvalidated until a comparative prototype and customer interviews establish incremental value beyond existing CI, tracing, evaluation, and runtime-policy products.

## Next frontier

Build a small benign verifier-backed testbed. Keep the exploration parent pool separate from the production incumbent. Use fixed budgets, independent repetitions, an untouched final set, and policy-compliant success as a separate metric. Measure cost per accepted improvement and per held-out gain. If existing tools provide equivalent evidence, narrow or abandon the wedge.

## Source quality

Primary arXiv full texts were inspected. Confidence is high in the reported numbers and stated limitations, moderate in generalization beyond the authors' environments, and low in any commercial-gap claim. The commercial thesis remains a hypothesis.
