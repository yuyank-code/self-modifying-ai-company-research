# Deep Research Pass — 2026-09-22

## Executive finding

Today's pass changes the protocol thesis from a generic Common Evolution State Machine toward an **Evolution Runtime Standard** with three newly explicit primitives:

1. **Evolution Budget** — a bounded allocation across search, replay, evaluation, security and human review.
2. **Evaluator Independence Class** — evaluator provenance and independence are part of the acceptance contract, not metadata.
3. **Evolution Compatibility Layer** — evolving agents/harnesses must expose stable interfaces so an ecosystem can evolve independently of any one foundation model.

Fresh evidence also shows the market is rapidly moving toward open infrastructure for agent evolution: Salesforce AI Research's Beagle is explicitly positioning itself as a unified platform for evaluating/evolving agent harnesses, with DarwinX as an initial evolution algorithm. Its published benchmark table reports substantial gains on Terminal-Bench 2.1, Terminal-World, WebArena-Infinity and SWE-bench Verified, while the project explicitly separates evaluation from evolution. This is direct competitive evidence against building a proprietary-only evolution framework.

## Fresh research findings

### 1. Beagle/DarwinX is a major competitive signal

Salesforce AI Research's open-source Beagle describes itself as a framework to evaluate and evolve agent harnesses at scale. It exposes an evaluate/evolve split and supports verifier-backed tasks. Its public benchmark table reports DarwinX improvements including +7.7 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1, +7.3 on Terminal-World, +49.5 on WebArena-Infinity in one reported setup, and +3.4 on SWE-bench Verified. These are vendor-reported results and require independent replication; they should not be treated as settled SOTA claims.

Strategic consequence: the company cannot rely on “we have an agent-evolution engine” as its moat. The moat must sit in evaluation integrity, cross-provider compatibility, production evidence, improvement economics, and accumulated improvement intelligence.

### 2. A-Evolve makes the ecosystem competition broader

A-EVO-Lab now describes A-Evolve as universal infrastructure for self-improving agents and publishes an evolving-agent stack spanning harness evolution, adaptive auto-harnessing and benchmark infrastructure. Its repository also states a key negative finding: evolver capability and harness benefit are decoupled, and stronger evolvers do not monotonically produce larger harness gains. This supports the earlier decision to keep capability budget on the task-solving agent and treat the evolver as a separately benchmarked component.

Strategic consequence: the protocol should model **Evolver** and **Solver** as separate identities with separate capability/evaluation records.

### 3. Living-Harness strengthens the persistent-update model

Living-Harness frames self-evolution as converting each completed trajectory and evaluator signal into posterior evidence for bounded harness updates. This supports treating post-deployment trajectory data as an input to controlled evolution rather than only as observability logs.

New requirement: every production trajectory that is eligible for evolution must carry a provenance chain identifying the exact harness, model, tools, evaluator and policy version that generated it.

### 4. OpenAI's internal R&D evidence strengthens the R&D-automation wedge

OpenAI's September 6 research-acceleration report describes coding agents reshaping researchers' daily work, increasing experimentation and changing the type of work researchers perform. OpenAI presents this as a measurable change in model-development velocity rather than autonomous RSI.

This reinforces the distinction:
- AI-assisted R&D = demonstrated
- AI-driven harness/model optimization = increasingly demonstrated
- autonomous open-ended RSI = not established

The commercial wedge can therefore be sold on measurable R&D/engineering acceleration without making an RSI claim.

### 5. OpenAI's policy position creates demand for a safety/control layer

OpenAI's September 9 policy document explicitly discusses preparing for recursive self-improvement and calls for mandatory capability-based safety requirements and independent safety-assessment infrastructure. This is strategic evidence that evaluation and governance may become infrastructure markets rather than optional consulting services.

### 6. Agentic commerce exposes the action-boundary problem

Reuters reports that major banks are warning about risks from AI shopping agents, including scams, incorrect purchases, payment-method manipulation and data-privacy problems, and recommends disclosure, transparency, data safeguards and interoperability. This is outside the core coding-agent market but directly validates the Authority Plane thesis: high-value irreversible actions require explicit boundaries and auditability.

### 7. Amazon/Meta conflict is evidence that browser-agent interoperability is not settled

Reporting today indicates Amazon is blocking Meta's Muse agent from accessing its marketplace, citing unauthorized AI access, credential/data concerns and terms-of-use issues. This is a commercial signal that browser-agent access cannot be assumed to be a neutral protocol layer. API-first access, merchant consent, identity and explicit agent authorization are likely to matter for the eventual ecosystem.

### New invariants

1. **Evolver/Solver separation:** the component proposing a mutation must be independently identifiable from the component solving the benchmark/task.
2. **Evaluator independence class:** acceptance records must classify evaluators as self, cross-agent, customer-owned, vendor-owned, or independent-third-party.
3. **Budget conservation:** an evolution run must declare a total budget before candidate search; post-hoc budget expansion is itself an auditable event.
4. **Scope monotonicity:** a candidate cannot increase its own EvolutionScope.
5. **Authority monotonicity:** a candidate cannot grant itself new permissions.
6. **Evaluator immutability during acceptance:** the candidate cannot modify the evaluator or acceptance policy used to judge it.
7. **Replay reproducibility:** every accepted change must be replayable from immutable artifacts or have an explicit non-reproducibility classification.

## New product hypothesis

### Evolution Control Plane for existing agents

The strongest near-term commercial product remains an infrastructure layer rather than a new agent:

- connect an existing coding/enterprise agent;
- capture trajectories;
- diagnose recurring failures;
- generate scoped improvements;
- run low-cost search/replay;
- invoke protected independent evaluation;
- calculate performance/capability/authority/persistence deltas;
- create an Evolution Commit;
- canary and rollback;
- learn which operators work for which failure classes.

The differentiator is **verified improvement per dollar**, not raw benchmark score.

## New moat hypothesis

Beagle/A-Evolve reduce the plausibility of an evolution-engine-only moat. The strongest candidate proprietary asset is now the **Improvement Intelligence Graph**:

failure class → reference trajectory → operator → candidate → evaluator disagreement → accepted/rejected outcome → deployment result → cost → transfer scope

This graph can learn which improvement strategies work across models, tools, organizations and domains while retaining provenance and security constraints.

## Economics update

### New metric: Verified Improvement Yield (VIY)

VIY = accepted production improvements / total evolution budget.

Track both:
- VIY per dollar
- VIY per protected evaluator call
- VIY per human review hour
- VIY per wall-clock day

The objective is to discover whether a platform can produce more *trusted* improvement than a human engineering team for the same budget.

## Experiments added

### E105 — Beagle/DarwinX independent replication
Reproduce reported improvements on at least two benchmarks with frozen test sets and independent evaluators.

### E106 — Evolver/Solver decoupling
Hold the solver fixed and vary the evolver; then hold the evolver fixed and vary the solver. Measure whether evolver quality predicts final harness benefit.

### E107 — Evolution budget frontier
Compare fixed-budget search with adaptive allocation across search/replay/trust compute.

### E108 — Evaluator independence sensitivity
Measure how acceptance rates and false-positive improvements change as evaluator independence increases.

### E109 — Scope inflation attack
Attempt to cause an evolution candidate to broaden its permitted mutation scope. Expected result: external policy layer rejects all scope increases.

### E110 — Cross-provider Evolution Commit portability
Create the same evolution record for OpenAI/Anthropic/open-model agents and test whether artifacts/evidence remain interpretable across providers.

### E111 — Browser-agent consent boundary
Compare API-first, MCP/tool and browser paths under explicit merchant/website consent and measure action reliability, authorization failures and security exposure.

### E112 — Improvement Intelligence transfer
Train operator-selection priors on one domain and test transfer to another while tracking degradation and provenance.

## Company formation implication

The first company should not attempt to compete with NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI or Anthropic on foundation models. It should build a neutral infrastructure layer that works across them.

Initial wedge:
**Adaptive Agent CI/CD + Evidence Gateway for coding/DevOps agents.**

Expansion sequence:
1. coding-agent evolution
2. enterprise workflow-agent evolution
3. cross-model/harness compatibility
4. evaluation/provenance infrastructure
5. marketplace for verified skills/operators
6. adaptive application/database infrastructure
7. model-component evolution
8. eventually model/weight evolution if economically and scientifically justified

This path preserves a credible route to a CUDA-like platform: stable evolution contracts and tooling across changing underlying intelligence providers.

## Cognitive Holobiont connection

Today's evidence supports an engineering analogy between a distributed adaptive software platform and a collective adaptive cognitive system: local agents produce experience, shared state preserves useful adaptations, evaluation enforces stability, and bounded evolution changes the collective system over time. This remains an analogy; no current evidence establishes consciousness, biological equivalence, or a realized Cognitive Holobiont.

## Contradictions logged

1. **More powerful evolver vs better evolution:** current evidence indicates non-monotonicity; evolver strength does not guarantee harness benefit.
2. **More candidates vs more improvement:** evaluation can be the bottleneck, making extra generation economically useless.
3. **More autonomy vs more value:** external enterprise reports and production anecdotes indicate that autonomous deployment can create productivity regressions when workflow integration is poor.
4. **Browser universality vs platform control:** merchant/operator restrictions show that browser agents cannot assume universal access.
