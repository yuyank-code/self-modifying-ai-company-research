# Deep Research Log — 2026-10-09

## Executive conclusion
Today's new frontier is claim-aware, cost-aware evidence allocation. Evaluation should be planned around the claim being made, not run as one generic suite for every decision.

## New findings
1. A Sep. 30, 2026 study of 22 agent benchmarks reports that fixed model+harness systems can be ranked more reliably than underlying models, and that more tasks do not resolve uncertainty dominated by scaffold coverage. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00651
2. A 2026 study reports that mid-difficulty tasks with historical pass rates around 30–70% can preserve rankings while reducing task counts by 44–70% in its tested settings; absolute-score prediction is less stable under shift. This supports shortlisting, not production acceptance. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23749
3. A September 2026 paper identifies a double measurement confound: scaffold decisions and scorer validity both affect reported agent capability. It recommends ground-truth scoring and tail-risk reporting. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09218
4. RHO uses prior trajectories and self-preference to improve harnesses, reporting SWE-Bench Pro pass rate from 59% to 78% after one round without external grading. This is promising search evidence, not independent proof of generalization. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05922
5. RRSI's public Apache-2.0 code includes edit history, candidate worktrees, leakage screening, noise-adjusted acceptance, cost-aware selection and pruning. Search roles use Claude Opus 4.8 via Vertex AI; one domain uses Gemini 3.5 Flash as judge. https://github.com/google-research/rrsi
6. IBM's ST-WebAgentBench reports average policy-compliant completion below two-thirds of nominal completion for three open agents, reinforcing that unsafe success must not count as success. https://research.ibm.com/publications/st-webagentbench-a-benchmark-for-evaluating-safety-and-trustworthiness-in-web-agents--1
7. MCP tool annotations are hints, not enforcement; authorization must be enforced by the host/runtime. https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-03-16-tool-annotations/
8. Palantir AIP Evolve is generally available; its documented example uses ten selected cases and reports 65% lower compute cost while retaining those cases. This proves a commercial optimization workflow exists, not broad generalization. https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/aip-evolve/overview
9. NVIDIA announced its Open Agent Safety Platform on Sep. 28, 2026, combining OpenShell runtime controls with a Sentry reference design. This strengthens the decision to integrate with security substrates and differentiate above them. https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Launches-Open-Agent-Safety-Platform-to-Secure-Agents-From-Testing-to-Deployment/

## New synthesis
Use two stages: (A) inexpensive candidate search/shortlisting, where history and self-preference may help; (B) independent acceptance, with hidden tasks, claim-specific scaffold coverage, ground-truth checks, repeated trials where relevant, and non-compensatory policy/security gates. A task subset that preserves ranking does not necessarily calibrate absolute production success.

## Company implication
Sharpen the wedge to a cross-platform Evidence Budget Planner and portable Evidence Contract: declare the claim, estimate dominant uncertainty across task/scaffold/scorer/runtime/seed, allocate the evaluation budget, then record why promotion was or was not justified. The moat is not the schema alone; it must come from calibrated decisions, integrations, and production-validated failure-to-repair knowledge.

## Evidence level and limitations
The cited studies are primary preprints or benchmark/vendor documentation. Reported results are setup-specific and require replication. No experiments were executed in this pass; the next work is to implement and test the planner.
