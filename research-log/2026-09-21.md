# Research Log — 2026-09-21

## Executive conclusion

Today's pass materially changes the protocol design. New September 2026 work on HarnessEvolve, SafeEvolve, APEX, modular harness evolution, and theoretical limits shows that reliable self-evolution needs explicit separation of **reference trajectories, candidate generation, quality filtering, protected evaluation, and promotion**. The most important new principle is that mutation scope must be treated as a first-class typed constraint: broadening the search space can increase credit-assignment and evaluation difficulty faster than it increases verified improvement.

The working company thesis remains **trusted adaptive software infrastructure**, but the platform protocol should now distinguish a cheap QualityGate from protected Evaluation and should explicitly model reference trajectories and evaluator independence.

## Fresh evidence

### 1. HarnessEvolve: reference trajectories improve credit assignment and stability

HarnessEvolve, submitted September 1, explicitly separates execution, evaluation, optimization and gating into independent modules. It uses reference trajectories to align failed executions with successful paths, then clusters error signals to identify systematic failure patterns. It uses a quality gate to reject leakage/prompt-bloat style changes and a performance gate that requires improvement without degrading recent batches, followed by held-out epoch validation.

This is directly relevant to the protocol because terminal success/failure alone is too coarse for deciding which mutation caused an improvement. Reference trajectories should therefore become an optional first-class protocol object, with provenance and leakage controls.

Source: Wen Jiang et al., “HarnessEvolve: Learning from Reference Trajectories for Reliable Agent Self-Evolution,” arXiv, submitted 2026-09-01. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00829
Source quality: Primary research paper. Confidence: Medium-high pending independent replication.

### 2. SafeEvolve: safety itself can become an evolving harness-policy loop

SafeEvolve couples bounded harness updates with policy learning. On the harness side it turns safety evidence from trajectories into component-level updates across safety prompts and hierarchical skills; on the policy side it uses SFT and RL to train the model to use those evolved artifacts. On the reported Qwen3.5-4B AgentDojo experiment, it reports a 3x reduction in attack success rate while increasing benign utility from 59.79% to 61.86%.

The important architectural implication is that safety adaptation can be represented as a governed evolution transaction rather than a one-time alignment phase. However, policy-level mutation raises the authority and evaluation burden substantially compared with harness-only changes.

Source: Qinghua Mao et al., “SafeEvolve: Harness-Policy Co-Evolution from Agent Experience for Safety Alignment,” arXiv, submitted 2026-09-02. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02786
Source quality: Primary research paper. Confidence: Medium pending independent replication and broader attack suites.

### 3. Theoretical limits: more candidates do not necessarily mean better guarantees

“Safe Harness Self-Evolution: A Theoretical Analysis of Feasibility and Limits” separates modification generation from certification and selection. Its theoretical analysis shows that generating more candidates does not necessarily improve the probability of a successfully certified update when evaluation is the bottleneck. It also derives that worst-case evaluation cost diverges as expected reward approaches its upper bound and that successful updates do not imply that further improvements remain possible.

This is a major economic result for the company thesis: the scarce resource may become **trusted evaluation**, not candidate generation.

Source: Qianshu Cai et al., “Safe Harness Self-Evolution: A Theoretical Analysis of Feasibility and Limits,” arXiv, submitted 2026-09-08. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08175
Source quality: Primary theoretical paper. Confidence: Medium-high for the mathematical claims under its assumptions; external validity depends on distributional assumptions.

### 4. APEX: multi-axis co-evolution can outperform single-axis harness optimization

APEX evolves three layers: harness failure patches, reusable behavioral principles, and workflow topology. In its reported production-agent experiment it used 114 real task traces across 18 days and reported a health score of 0.570 versus 0.300 baseline, with six reusable principles and a selected workflow topology score of 0.900. It also reports a low-cost run using four LLM calls on a local Qwen2.5-Coder 32B instance.

This is important but currently weaker evidence than controlled benchmark studies because it is a specific production-agent setup and the health score is custom. It nevertheless supports testing whether carefully bounded multi-axis evolution beats single-axis evolution when mutation scopes and evaluators are explicit.

Source: Ya-Chuan Chen et al., “APEX: Adaptive Principle EXtraction,” arXiv, submitted 2026-06-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15363
Source quality: Primary research paper. Confidence: Medium-low until reproduced on independent benchmarks.

### 5. One Recipe, Many Harnesses: evolved harnesses encode both transferable abstractions and local ecosystem knowledge

This study evaluates one evolution recipe across eight programming languages and three base models. It reports that the shared core of evolved harnesses transfers, while language-ecosystem-specific machinery often requires native re-evolution. It also identifies null regions where evolution does not produce gains.

This creates an important new distinction for the platform: some improvement artifacts are **universal operators/principles**, while others are **environment-specific compensators**. The Improvement Graph must therefore represent transfer scope rather than treating every skill/harness artifact as universally reusable.

Source: Siqi Yang et al., “One Recipe, Many Harnesses: What Self-Evolution Encodes Across Languages and Models,” arXiv, submitted 2026-08-10. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10178
Source quality: Primary research paper. Confidence: Medium-high for reported experiments; broader transfer requires more environments.

### 6. Current frontier-lab R&D automation remains supervised

Anthropic reports that, as of August 2026, Claude leads 26% of measured AI R&D work and that more than 90% is at or above a collaboration level, but no measured subset is fully autonomous. Anthropic also notes methodological problems including the absence of common cross-lab definitions and the fact that the lab's own model participates in judging the work.

This is strong evidence for rapid AI-assisted R&D acceleration, not evidence of open-ended RSI.

Source: Anthropic, “Measurements for understanding the pace of AI development inside frontier labs,” 2026. https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development
Source quality: Primary frontier-lab measurement. Confidence: High for Anthropic's own figures; medium for generalization.

## New synthesis

### A. Mutation scope is now a first-class protocol object

Prior work already separated evolution depth. Today's evidence adds **scope breadth**.

Evolution must specify both:
- how deep the mutation is allowed to reach (prompt, skill, workflow, code, harness, policy, weights, etc.)
- how broad the mutation can be across modules simultaneously.

The platform should assume that broader mutation increases search and credit-assignment complexity unless measured otherwise.

### B. Reference trajectories should sit before candidate generation

The proposed state machine is therefore refined to:

AgentArtifact → HarnessSnapshot → EvolutionScope → ReferenceTrajectories → Operator → Candidate → RuntimeEvidence → QualityGate → Evaluation → Delta → Acceptance → EvolutionCommit → Lineage → Canary → ProductionOutcome → ReplayDataset.

### C. QualityGate and protected Evaluation must remain separate

QualityGate is cheap and structural. It catches:
- leakage
- prompt bloat
- malformed artifacts
- scope violations
- obvious regressions
- dependency/provenance errors.

Protected Evaluation answers the harder question:
**did this candidate actually improve generalizable behavior under independent evidence?**

Passing QualityGate is never equivalent to passing Evaluation.

### D. Evaluator independence becomes explicit metadata

Evaluation records should classify evaluator independence:
1. self-evaluator
2. same-provider external evaluator
3. heterogeneous model evaluator
4. customer-owned evaluator
5. independent third-party evaluator.

A production promotion should not rely solely on self-evaluation when the mutation can affect the evaluator, benchmark, authority boundary or persistent state.

## Contradictions added

1. **More candidates vs better guarantees:** theory says candidate volume cannot compensate for an evaluation bottleneck.
2. **Single-axis vs multi-axis evolution:** APEX reports gains from co-evolution, while modular-evolution evidence warns that unrestricted joint mutation can underperform. The resolution may be that multi-axis evolution is useful only when mutation scopes remain typed and separately attributable.
3. **Universal vs local improvement:** evolved harnesses contain a transferable abstract playbook but also environment-specific ecosystem machinery. The platform should track transfer scope rather than assume universal reuse.
4. **Safety co-evolution vs safety immutability:** SafeEvolve suggests safety policies can improve through experience, but this raises the risk that the system evolves the safety boundary itself. Safety-policy mutations therefore require stricter evaluator quorum and authority controls than ordinary performance mutations.

## Company implications

### Product wedge remains
Adaptive Agent CI/CD + Evidence Gateway for coding/DevOps and enterprise workflow agents.

### New differentiator
The platform should market **controlled improvement**, not generic autonomous agents:
- typed mutation scope
- reference-trajectory diagnostics
- cheap quality gates
- protected evaluation
- evaluator-independence metadata
- Evolution Commit
- canary/rollback
- production outcome learning.

### Moat hypothesis update
The strongest proprietary asset remains the longitudinal improvement dataset, but it should be decomposed into:
- failure patterns
- causal/reference trajectories
- operator performance
- scope-to-outcome statistics
- evaluator disagreement patterns
- deployment outcomes
- transferability metadata.

This is closer to an **Improvement Intelligence Graph** than a generic memory store.

### Economics
The theoretical result strengthens the need to optimize **Verified Improvement Cost (VIC)**. Candidate generation is increasingly cheap; protected evaluation can become the limiting cost. The platform's intelligence should therefore learn when not to search further.

## Cognitive Holobiont boundary

Today's findings strengthen the engineering analogy around modular specialization, shared causal experience, bounded adaptation, and homeostatic evaluation/rollback. They do not provide evidence for consciousness, biology, or a realized Cognitive Holobiont.

## Exact next frontier

Implement the local Common Evolution State Machine prototype around the smallest useful typed `EvolutionScope`, `ReferenceTrajectory`, `QualityGate`, `Evaluation`, `Delta`, and `EvolutionCommit` schemas. The prototype should first support prompt/skill evolution, then coding/harness evolution, then replay-assisted search-policy evolution. The research question is no longer whether these systems can be described by one abstraction; it is whether the abstraction can execute real improvement jobs without weakening evaluation or authority boundaries.
