# Research Log — 2026-10-19

## Focus
Diagnosis-first self-improvement, speculative successor search, behavioral specifications, and evaluator qualification.

## Executive conclusion
The newest evidence suggests three distinct engineering problems: explore modifications efficiently; prevent local changes from destabilizing dependent components; and qualify the evaluator before its score is used to approve a release. These functions should be measured separately.

## SpecForge
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15708 (submitted 2026-10-11).
SpecForge refines natural-language behavioral specifications that guide code generation. It uses a Pareto archive, dependency-aware entity selection, and error-diversified candidate expansion. The paper reports a 94.9% solve rate with Claude-4.5-Sonnet on DevEval+ and a 5.7 percentage-point average gain over its strongest baseline across five backbones. It reports downstream transfer to cross-language translation and new-feature implementation.

Critical limitation stated by the authors: recursion is over specifications, not over the improver; the search procedure remains fixed. The evaluation covers 20 Python modules and requires a reference implementation to validate generated tests. This is bounded external-context refinement, not open-ended recursive self-improvement.

## SpecRSI
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16491 (submitted 2026-10-12).
SpecRSI separates successor drafting from full verification. Verification-Aware Tree Search allocates resources between edits and checks; Group Verification Optimization updates the improver while holding the verification contract fixed. The paper reports gains over Janus and MetaRSI-v1 across tested tasks, including SWE-Pro coding validation of 69.4% versus 64.3% for HGM with a 20.8% lower target-cost index. It also reports a 5.7% lower lifecycle cost index than MetaRSI-v1 in one comparison.

Caveats: results are author-reported and not independently reproduced here; scores and costs are profile-specific. More planning can improve raw gain while lowering net return. Predictions may schedule checks but must not count as evidence; only complete, admissible verification records should authorize adoption.

## AGO AI Quality Gate
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01218 (submitted 2026-10-01).
AGO proposes four-state release decisions, layered deterministic and model-based checks, a stratified beta-binomial gate, and mandatory meta-evaluation of LLM judges. On RAGBench samples, reported adherence AUROC is 0.603 for GPT-4.1-nano and 0.783 for GPT-4o. Even the stronger judge varies by domain. The low-cost judge produced well-formed output while discriminating only slightly above chance.

The gate study reports fewer unsafe promotions under regression, but this trades off against review/block rates and throughput. Enterprise engagement data are proprietary; synthetic operating characteristics are not universal production rates.

## Competitive implication
Arize (https://arize.com/) and Galileo (https://galileo.ai/) already market tracing, evaluation, failure analysis, guardrails, and continual-improvement workflows. AGO overlaps with evaluator qualification and evidence-based release gates. A generic dashboard, trace viewer, or quality score is not a defensible wedge.

## Revised synthesis
Use two planes. The exploration plane proposes changes, maintains branches, and predicts expected return. The promotion plane uses an independent, versioned evaluator; immutable candidate identity; complete scoped checks; policy vetoes; uncertainty-aware decisions; and an explicit REVIEW/INSUFFICIENT-EVIDENCE state. Predictions are not evidence. The candidate generator must not change hidden holdout sets or acceptance rules.

## Evidence classification
- Demonstrated within cited studies: bounded specification refinement; budget-aware speculative search; judge quality varies by domain; confidence intervals can change conclusions.
- Promising: integrating these ideas into a common change-evidence protocol.
- Unproven: a generic certification layer is commercially differentiated; methods generalize to all agent domains; recursive improvement compounds indefinitely; customers will pay for a standalone product.

## Next research frontier
1. Implement a deterministic testbed with two agent runtimes and a fixed change-artifact schema.
2. Compare sequential search, speculative drafting with complete verification, and greedy single-path optimization at matched total cost.
3. Qualify a low-cost judge against independent reference labels per domain.
4. Test output coupling with dependency-linked modules and unseen tests.
5. Conduct 10–15 buyer interviews before expanding scope.

## Source quality
All three core sources are primary preprints. Results are promising but have not been independently reproduced in this project. No prototype experiment or customer interview was executed in this pass.
